Raipur, Chhattisgarh

Khurmi

Wheat flour, jaggery, and ghee, shaped into discs and deep-fried, made at home especially during the Pola and Teej festivals.

Arpa Pairi Ke Dhar — Mamta Chandrakar

Landmark

Mahant Ghasidas Memorial Museum

History

Founded by Brahma Deo Rai of the Kalachuri dynasty, which ruled the region historically known as Dakshina Kosala. Raipur became the capital of Chhattisgarh when the state was carved out of Madhya Pradesh on November 1, 2000.

Places to visit
  • Mahant Ghasidas Memorial Museum
  • Swami Vivekananda Sarovar (Budha Talab)
  • Purkhouti Muktangan, Naya Raipur

Surat, Gujarat

Ghari

A rich, layered sweet of mawa, ghee, and dry fruits set on a crisp base, traditionally eaten in Surat on Chandni Padvo, the full-moon night after Diwali.

Vaishnav Jan To — written by 15th-century poet-saint Narsinh Mehta, sung by Lata Mangeshkar

Landmark

Surat Fort

History

One of the busiest ports on the Indian Ocean trade route through the Mughal era, Surat became the site of the British East India Company's first Indian trading factory in 1608. It later grew into a major center for textiles and diamond polishing, earning it the names "Silk City" and "Diamond City."

Places to visit
  • Surat Fort (Old Fort)
  • Dutch Garden
  • Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel Museum

Kozhikode, Kerala

Kozhikodan Halwa

A soft, chewy, jelly-like halwa of wheat or rice starch, ghee, sugar, and cashews, famously sold along Kozhikode's "Sweet Meat Street" in black, white, and red varieties.

Omanathinkal Kidavo — 19th-century lullaby by Irayimman Thampi, sung by K. S. Chithra

Landmark

Kappad Beach

History

Kozhikode (Calicut) was the seat of the powerful Zamorin dynasty and a key node in the Indian Ocean spice trade for centuries. In 1498, Vasco da Gama landed at nearby Kappad beach, opening the first direct sea route between Europe and India.

Places to visit
  • Kappad Beach
  • Kozhikode Beach
  • Beypore (traditional Uru boat-building port)

Puri, Odisha

Khaja

A crisp, flaky, multi-layered fried pastry dipped in sugar syrup, one of the traditional sweets offered as prasad at the Jagannath Temple.

Bande Utkala Janani — Odisha's state anthem, written by Kantakabi Laxmikanta Mohapatra

Landmark

Jagannath Temple

History

Puri is one of Hinduism's Char Dham pilgrimage sites, home to the Jagannath Temple built in the 12th century under the Eastern Ganga dynasty. It hosts the annual Rath Yatra chariot festival, one of the largest religious processions in the world.

Places to visit
  • Jagannath Temple
  • Puri Beach
  • Konark Sun Temple (nearby)

Almora, Uttarakhand

Bal Mithai

Khoya cooked with cane sugar until deep brown, cut into cubes and rolled in tiny sugar-coated poppy-seed balls, a specialty of Kumaon's Almora district.

Bedu Pako Baro Masa — traditional Kumaoni folk song, now the Kumaon Regiment's regimental song

Landmark

Kasar Devi Temple

History

Founded in 1568 by Kalyan Chand of the Chand dynasty, Almora was the seat of the Kumaon kingdom into the late 18th century, later held by Gorkha forces until the British took it in the 1814 Anglo-Gorkha War. Set on a Himalayan ridge, it remains the cultural capital of Kumaon.

Places to visit
  • Kasar Devi Temple
  • Bright End Corner
  • Binsar Wildlife Sanctuary

Panaji, Goa

Bebinca

A 7-to-16-layer Indo-Portuguese cake of flour, sugar, ghee, coconut milk, and egg yolk, baked one thin layer at a time and traditionally made for Christmas.

Bebdo — Lorna Cordeiro, composed by Chris Perry, traditional Goan Konkani song

Landmark

Basilica of Bom Jesus

History

Panaji appears in an 1059 CE Kadamba-dynasty copper plate; its fort was stormed by the Portuguese in 1510. After an epidemic devastated Old Goa, the Portuguese rebuilt around Panaji as the new capital of Portuguese India in the 18th century, and it has been Goa's state capital since 1987.

Places to visit
  • Basilica of Bom Jesus (Old Goa)
  • Fort Aguada
  • Miramar Beach

Srinagar, Jammu & Kashmir

Firni

A ground-rice milk pudding flavoured with rose water or kewra, known locally as "firin," the traditional closing dessert of Kashmir's Wazwan feast.

Bumbro — Mission Kashmir, sung by Sunidhi Chauhan, based on a traditional Kashmiri folk melody

Landmark

Dal Lake

History

Present-day Srinagar traces to Parvarapura, founded in the 6th century by King Pravarasena II at the base of Hari Parbat. Sultanate rulers built the Jamia Masjid in 1402, and Mughal emperors later laid out the Shalimar and Nishat gardens along the lake.

Places to visit
  • Dal Lake
  • Shalimar Bagh
  • Shankaracharya Temple

Puducherry

Crème Brûlée

A caramelized-sugar custard that stuck around after 280 years as a French colonial outpost. No other Indian city's "local sweet" is French, and that's exactly the point.

Kakkai Chiraginile — written by Subramania Bharati, sung by K. J. Yesudas

Landmark

Matrimandir, Auroville

History

Ruled by the Pallavas, Cholas, and Pandyas before French trader François Martin founded a settlement in 1674, Puducherry stayed France's chief Indian colony until administration passed to India in 1954. Poet-freedom fighter Subramania Bharati lived here in exile from 1908 to 1918.

Places to visit
  • Auroville (Matrimandir)
  • Sri Aurobindo Ashram
  • Promenade Beach / French Quarter

Imphal, Manipur

Chak-hao Kheer

A rice pudding made from Manipur's aromatic, dark-purple chak-hao rice, cooked with milk, coconut, and raisins for festivals and special occasions.

Khongjom Parva (Moirang Kangleirol) — traditional Pena ballad narrating the Khamba-Thoibi legend

Landmark

Kangla Fort

History

Imphal was the seat of the Meitei Ningthouja dynasty kings for centuries, centered on Kangla Fort, until the British took control after the 1891 Anglo-Manipur War. In 1944 it was the site of the Battle of Imphal, where Allied forces halted Japan's attempted invasion of India.

Places to visit
  • Kangla Fort
  • Loktak Lake
  • Ima Keithel (the all-women's market)

Guwahati, Assam

Til Pitha

A rice-flour crepe rolled around roasted sesame and jaggery, made especially during Assam's Magh Bihu harvest festival.

Mayabini Ratir Bukut — Zubeen Garg

Landmark

Kamakhya Temple

History

Guwahati, on the banks of the Brahmaputra, was known in antiquity as Pragjyotishpura and served as capital of the ancient Kamarupa kingdom. It is today Assam's largest city and the gateway to Northeast India, home to the Kamakhya Temple, one of the subcontinent's oldest Shakti Peetha shrines.

Places to visit
  • Kamakhya Temple
  • Umananda Island
  • Assam State Museum

Tirupati, Andhra Pradesh

Tirupati Laddu

A ghee-roasted gram-flour laddu with cashews, cardamom, and raisins, prepared daily as prasadam at the Tirumala temple and awarded a Geographical Indication tag in 2009.

Sri Venkatesa Suprabhatam — M. S. Subbulakshmi

Landmark

Tirumala Venkateswara Temple

History

Tirupati grew around the Tirumala Venkateswara Temple, one of the world's most-visited religious sites, with inscriptions and endowments dating to the Pallava, Chola, and Vijayanagara periods.

Places to visit
  • Tirumala Venkateswara Temple
  • Sri Venkateswara Zoological Park
  • Kapila Theertham

Gwalior, Madhya Pradesh

Gajak

A crisp, dry winter sweet of hand-beaten sesame seeds and jaggery, with Gwalior and the neighbouring Morena-Bhind belt famous for it, traditionally eaten around Makar Sankranti.

Rain Ragas: Megh and Miyan ki Malhar — Ustad Amjad Ali Khan on sarod, Gwalior's own gharana lineage

Landmark

Gwalior Fort

History

Gwalior grew around its hilltop fort, ruled successively by the Tomars, Mughals, and the Maratha Scindia dynasty. It is the seat of the Gwalior Gharana of Hindustani classical music and home to Tansen's tomb; UNESCO named it a "City of Music" in 2023.

Places to visit
  • Gwalior Fort (Man Singh Palace)
  • Jai Vilas Palace
  • Tansen's Tomb

Ranchi, Jharkhand

Tilkut

Pounded sesame seeds bound into a jaggery or sugar batter and pressed into discs, made for Makar Sankranti across Jharkhand, Bihar, and eastern UP.

Nagpur Kar Kora — Madhu Mansuri Hasmukh, Padma Shri-winning voice of the Jharkhand statehood movement

Landmark

Jagannath Temple, Ranchi

History

Ranchi grew into a colonial administrative hub after the South-West Frontier Agency set up headquarters here in 1833, and later served as Bihar's summer capital. It became Jharkhand's capital when the state was carved out on November 15, 2000, and is nicknamed the "City of Waterfalls" for cascades like Hundru Falls on its outskirts.

Places to visit
  • Jagannath Temple
  • Rock Garden (Kanke Dam)
  • Hundru Falls

Kurukshetra, Haryana

Panjiri

Whole-wheat flour roasted in ghee with dried fruits and herbal gums, bound with unrefined sugar; a Haryana winter staple, also given to new mothers for its warming ingredients.

Teri Aakhya Ka Yo Kajal — Veer Dahiya, Haryanvi

Landmark

Brahma Sarovar

History

Kurukshetra, also called Dharmakshetra, is named after King Kuru and revered as the battlefield of the Mahabharata war, where Krishna delivered the Bhagavad Gita to Arjuna at Jyotisar. It remains a major pilgrimage site anchored by Brahma Sarovar, one of Asia's largest man-made sacred ponds.

Places to visit
  • Brahma Sarovar
  • Jyotisar
  • Sthaneshwar Mahadev Temple

Kullu, Himachal Pradesh

Bobaru

A deep-fried, flat wheat-flour sweet with jaggery and dry fruits, a Kullu valley specialty traditionally shared with family and neighbours the day after Lohri.

Teri Shangri Kullu Ri Deiye — Kuldeep Sharma, "Nati King"

Landmark

Raghunath Temple

History

The Kullu valley appears in the Ramayana, Mahabharata, and Puranas, and the Chinese monk Xuanzang described it around 634-635 CE as a fertile valley ringed by mountains and monasteries. In 1650, the idol of Raghunath was brought from Ayodhya and enshrined here, and it still anchors Kullu's famous Dussehra festival today.

Places to visit
  • Raghunath Temple
  • Naggar
  • Bijli Mahadev Temple
